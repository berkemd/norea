Palimpsest

Most archaeology apps show you a reconstruction and let you believe it. Whether Enclosure D at Göbekli Tepe ever had a roof is disputed; drawing it as though the question were settled is not a rendering choice, it is selling an opinion as knowledge. Palimpsest is built so that it cannot do that.

What it does differently

Every layer carries its source

A layer with no source cannot be created at all — the initialiser returns nothing, so the rule is enforced by the compiler rather than by discipline. Author, year and a lookup identifier sit under every claim, not in a credits screen.

Three levels of evidence, on screen

Measured: an excavation report and a date. Reconstruction: a published interpretation. Disputed: the same evidence read more than one way. The level is drawn as a shape rather than a colour, so it survives colour blindness — and it is the app's content, not its decoration.

No date is a point

Uncertainty cannot be zero here; the type refuses it. 9500 BCE ± 500 is drawn as a band beside 3200 BCE ± 100 on one axis, so which of the two is thinly known is seen rather than read.

The slider will not lie about time

A slider that gives every layer an equal share is easy to navigate but teaches a false scale. A slider proportional to real time teaches the scale but is awkward. Both are defensible, so the choice is yours — and whichever you pick, the real gap in years is written out underneath.

Computed is not measured

Solstice alignments are computed from the obliquity of the ecliptic for that site and that epoch, and the screen says so in as many words. Where a field survey exists it is drawn as a solid line beside the dashed computed one. Where the published claims contradict each other — Göbekli Tepe, Nabta Playa, Çatalhöyük — no angle is stored at all, and the app explains why instead of choosing a side.

What it will not do

  • It will not draw a disputed reconstruction as though it were settled.
  • It will not show a claim without its source.
  • It will not ask for a single permission, and it makes no network requests at all — this is checked in the code on every build, not merely promised here.

Eight places in version 1.0: Göbekli Tepe, Stonehenge, Newgrange, Maeshowe, Troy, Çatalhöyük, Nabta Playa and Karnak. iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch. Six languages. Free, with no advertising.
