Bestow

A good gift is not a product. It is proof of attention.

Every gift app stores products: a link, a wishlist row, an affiliate tag. But the source of a good gift was never a catalogue. It was the sentence someone said in passing.

The unit is a noticing

“She lets her coffee go cold — she mentioned a warmer.”

Each noticing carries who it is about, how sure you are (guessed, seen, said), when you noticed it, and how the thing would have to be got: made, ordered, hard to find, or only done together.

Nothing is invented

Every suggestion traces back to at least one noticing you wrote. There is no catalogue, no “popular gifts”, no affiliate link — a suggestion that pays a commission is not a suggestion. When you have noticed nothing yet, Bestow says so rather than filling the screen.

Reminders come by how long it takes to get

Not by the date. “Their birthday is tomorrow” is not a reminder, it is an accusation. Bestow counts backwards from the acquisition:

how it would be gotreminder lands
ordered10 days before
made3 weeks before
planned together6 weeks before
hard to find8 weeks before

If the occasion is too close for any of it, Bestow tells you that instead of suggesting something that cannot arrive in time.

What was measured

The naive generator knows the budget, the acquisition classes and the dates — everything except the rules. It still breaks one in nine situations out of ten.

What it will not do

  • No catalogue and no affiliate links.
  • Your contacts are never read. Names are typed by hand. A gift app has no business reading your address book, and a build gate fails the release if the Contacts framework appears at all.
  • No account, no server, no sync, no network.
  • No subscription. Something used a few times a year is not rented.
