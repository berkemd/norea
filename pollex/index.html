Pollex

Touch the wound on an anatomical 3D hand: see the tendon zone at that spot and the tendons, nerves and vessels that lie beneath it. For emergency, plastic, orthopaedic and hand surgery clinicians.

For iPhone and iPad. A free download with an optional Pollex Pro subscription. No account, no data collection, works offline.

Free for everyone

  • 3D wound map: a hand and forearm to turn; touch the skin, see the flexor or extensor zone for that finger and the structures within a few millimetres of the wound track, nearest first.
  • Examine this wound: the zone's checks one by one, each test shown on the 3D hand, your findings recorded as normal, abnormal or not tested.
  • Call summary: site and findings as plain text to read out or paste when you call the hand surgeon. Nothing is stored.
  • Sensation map: median, ulnar and radial territories, as the textbooks describe them.
  • Urgent pathways: high-pressure injection injury, purulent flexor tenosynovitis, traumatic amputation, compartment syndrome, bites and ring avulsion, each ending with when to call.
  • Repair, free: the nail bed guide by injury type, and the replantation guide with ischemia time, the amputated part and the order of repair.
  • The basic tests (FDS, FDP, two-point discrimination, perfusion) and the first lesson.

Pollex Pro

  • The 3D layer explorer: skin, sheaths, tendons, bones, nerves, arteries and veins, one layer at a time.
  • Every test in 3D: FPL, EPL, EDC and EIP, the Elson test, the resting cascade.
  • The Repair planners: fingertip by digit, angle and exposed bone; cover on the reconstructive ladder; nerve repair; with Atasoy, Kutler, Moberg, island, cross-finger and thenar flaps drawn step by step.
  • Every lesson, with its splint and imaging guide and questions that come back when they are due.

Monthly or yearly; the yearly plan starts with a one-week free trial for eligible accounts.

Built on sources

Every statement names its source beneath it: Essentials of Plastic Surgery (3rd ed.), Acute Management of Hand Injuries, and peer-reviewed papers. Pollex is for clinicians. It names no specific drug and gives no doses, does not diagnose, and does not replace your hospital's protocol or the hand surgeon's assessment.
