Koine

Every vocabulary app reports a number: 800 words learned. That number measures nothing. Which 800? Common in what kind of text? Enough to read anything? Koine answers the question a learner actually has.

What it does differently

Coverage, with a band

Your words cover 78% of this corpus — between 76% and 80%. Not one figure pretending to be exact. The band is not a caveat bolted onto the number; it is the answer.

Two kinds of uncertainty, kept apart

Part of the band is random: it narrows every time you answer. Part is systematic — it comes from not knowing your own forgetting curve, and more questions will never narrow it; only time will. Koine tells you which one dominates, so the advice stops being "review more" and becomes something you can act on.

The reward is the band narrowing

Answer one more word and your coverage barely moves: a single word carries a few thousandths. What changes is how sure the number is. In every other app progress means the number going up; here the animation is reserved for the band tightening, because that is the real progress.

Honesty with a date on it

Two thousand words answered twelve days ago give less coverage than three hundred answered today. Koine shows that rather than leaving old cards in a "learned" column forever.

The corpus is shown, not hidden

3,209,861 tokens of everyday Spanish sentences, measured by us. Not news prose — and the app says so, because the same words cover a different share of each. Proper names are 2.89% of the corpus and are counted separately: reading a name does not require learning it, but we never measured whether you recognise it, so that assumption is yours to switch on or off.

Thresholds with their sources

95% coverage for reading with a dictionary (Laufer 1989); 98% for reading unassisted (Hu & Nation 2000). When your band crosses a threshold, Koine says it cannot tell yet instead of rounding you over the line. And coverage is not comprehension: word order, idiom, grammar and background knowledge are not measured here, and the app says that too.

Not "learn 500 more words"

Which 500 changes everything. Koine ranks what to learn next by how much each word would add to your coverage, and shows the upper bound you would reach if you learned every one of them perfectly.

Version 1.0 is Spanish. More languages as their corpora are measured — not before. iPhone and iPad. Six languages of interface.

What it will not do

  • It will not report a number without the uncertainty on it.
  • It will not call a word "learned" and leave it there while you forget it.
  • It will not make a single network request. No account, no analytics, no identifier, and not one permission is requested — this is checked in the code on every build.
