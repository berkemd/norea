Eidos

A profile you can argue with. Every weight in it points at its evidence.

Eidos does not ask what you are good at. It works out what your evidence supports — and shows you the line from every number back to the thing that produced it.

Why the mark is three circles

Three faint circles are three independent sources. The bright form is only the region all three cover. Withdraw one source and the form shrinks; withdraw two and there is nothing left. That is the whole product in one drawing: there is no self sitting underneath the evidence, waiting to be revealed.

What is inside

ShowcaseA ranking where raw popularity carries low, capped weight, under-validated entries are held back, and one of the five visible slots is reserved for someone new. Each row shows the score it earned.
PersonasEvidence-bound profiles of public-domain figures. Feed one a batch of sources and the app tells you which concept moved, from what weight to what weight. A figure with no rights record is marked memorial only and is never represented.
Your selfAn adaptive interview. An answer becomes a claim; a claim becomes evidence only by climbing a validation ladder. An unclimbed answer carries no weight at all.
FidelityA 0–1000 score from five named components. It cannot be bought and it cannot be rushed — part of it is simply time.
EmployerProtected traits are stripped before anything is scored, and the stripped list is printed on screen. An unverified self is not ranked low; it is not in the pool.

What it does not do

No account. No server. No analytics. No notifications, no streaks, no badges. It asks for no device permission of any kind — not camera, not microphone, not location, not contacts. There is nothing to switch off, because nothing was switched on.
