Assay

Every other calorie app shows one number. That number is a guess dressed as a fact — and when it is wrong, you stop trusting the app. Assay shows the range.

What it does differently

A range, not a number

Every estimate carries a band, and the band is on screen. The oil under the plate is invisible; so is the portion under the rice. That uncertainty cannot be removed, so it is shown instead of hidden.

Two kinds of uncertainty, kept apart

Error that repeats in the same direction at every meal — the model reading oily food low — is systematic, and it accumulates across a day. Portion-reading noise is random, and it partly cancels out. Adding them the same way gets one of the two wrong. Assay adds systematic error linearly and random error in quadrature, and shows you which one dominates your day.

It says when it cannot tell

A classifier that reports its first guess without looking at the second has invented its own certainty. When two candidates are close in energy, the answer still holds and Assay gives it. When they are far apart — a salad and a slice of cake — no single answer is honest, so Assay names both and asks. The threshold is on the energy spread, not on the confidence number.

The cost in your life, not in calories

620 kcal means nothing to anyone. "23–31 minutes of your evening walk" does. If Assay has not measured your pace yet, it says so rather than quietly using a table.

A date range, not a date

"You will hit your goal in 5 weeks" swallows every uncertainty above it. Assay gives the earliest and the latest day, derived from your own daily balance. Beyond a year it says so instead of inventing a date. The 7700 kcal-per-kilogram figure it uses is a convention, not a law, so it carries its own ±12% band into the result.

What it will not do

  • It will not fake a number it did not measure.
  • It will not send your food log anywhere. The app makes no network requests at all — this is checked on every build, not merely promised.
  • It will not shame you. There is no streak to break.
